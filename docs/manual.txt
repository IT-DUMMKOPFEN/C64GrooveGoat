# C64 GrooveGoat 0.9b - Manual

C64 GrooveGoat is a tracker-style music editor for the SID sound chip of the Commodore 64. It is
based on GoatTracker 2 by Lasse Oorni (Cadaver) and the "Silver Fork" of Joel Ricci. It adds:

- songs with 1, 2, 4 or 8 SID chips (up to 24 voices) with free SID addresses
- a modern, resizable window with mouse support and a day and a night (C64 blue) theme
- an instrument library with more than 5000 instruments, search, favourites and preview
- a random instrument generator for bass, drums, woodwinds, guitars, brass, strings and more
- a graphical ADSR envelope display
- undo and redo, automatic backup copies and crash recovery
- export of SID files for multi-SID players and of runnable C64 programs with a player screen
- export of WAV (also every voice as a single file) and MP3 audio, and of MP4 videos with an
  oscilloscope for every voice

Author of C64 GrooveGoat: IT-Dummkopfen. Click the Info button in the toolbar for all credits.
Platforms: Windows 11 (x64), macOS on Apple Silicon (M chips) and Linux (AppImage, x86-64).

This manual is built into the program: click Manual in the keyboard reference (F12, Help button)
or press M there. Next to it, the History button (or H) opens a chapter about the history of C64
SID music: the chip, its two versions, the great composers and its legacy. Use the cursor keys, Page Up/Down, Home/End or the mouse wheel to scroll, LEFT
and RIGHT jump to the previous / next chapter, click a chapter on the left to go there, press F
to search for a word (F3 or N finds the next place) and ESC to close the manual.

## What you need to know

Familiarity with tracker programs, hexadecimal numbers and the SID chip helps a lot. All values
in the editor are hexadecimal ($00-$FF). The SID chip has three voices; each voice has an
oscillator (triangle, sawtooth, pulse and noise waveforms) and an ADSR envelope, and all voices
of one SID share a multimode filter and the master volume.

A song consists of:

- Orderlists: the order in which patterns are played, one orderlist per voice (channel) and
  per subtune.
- Patterns: single-voice note data (notes, instrument numbers and commands).
- Instruments: ADSR settings plus pointers into the tables.
- Tables: the wave, pulse, filter and speed tables, small step programs that create the
  actual sound of the instruments.

## Warnings

- Always save your songs as .sng or .ggt files. Exported SID, PRG, BIN, WAV, MP3 and MP4 files
  can not be loaded back into the editor.
- Even the reSID emulation is not identical to a real SID, especially when filters are used.
  Every real 6581 sounds a little different. Test important songs on real hardware or with a
  good emulator.
- The editor stops playing on error conditions: an illegal song restart position, a wrong
  order of orderlist commands, a gateoff timer value that is too high for the tempo, a jump
  onto a table row that contains a jump, or the pattern commands 0XY, 8XY and EXY used in the
  wavetable. Exporting such a song gives an error message.
- Delayed wavetable steps or "no wave change" (0X 00) in the first step of an instrument's
  wavetable are not supported and may cause missing notes.
- Songs with 4 or 8 SIDs can only be played by multi-SID capable players and emulators (for
  example VICE, JSIDPlay2, the Ultimate 64 or SIDBlaster/USBSID hardware with several chips).

## Status bar and volume

The bottom lines show the octave, edit/jam mode, autoadvance mode, note entry mode (Keys),
keyboard layout (Kbd), the step size, the Undo and Redo buttons, the play state and time, the
positions of the visible voices and the SID addresses.

The volume slider at the bottom right sets the output volume of the editor from 0% to 100%
(default 80%). Click or drag on the slider, or use the mouse wheel over it; a click on the
speaker symbol mutes and unmutes. The volume only affects listening in the editor, not exported
files. It is saved with the other settings. Volume changes are faded smoothly, so moving the
slider does not click.

## Welcome window

At startup a welcome window with the most important keys is shown. Close it with the Close
button, ENTER, SPACE or ESC. Uncheck "Show at startup" (click it or press S) if you do not want
to see it again.

## Keyboard layout (Kbd: EN / DE)

C64 GrooveGoat reads the notes from the position of the keys, like a piano, so note entry works
the same on every keyboard. Shortcuts, however, should follow the letters printed on the keys.
On a German (QWERTZ) keyboard the keys Z and Y are swapped compared to an English (QWERTY)
keyboard. The "Kbd" button in the status bar selects the layout:

|  Kbd: EN     English / international keyboards (QWERTY)
|  Kbd: DE     German keyboards (QWERTZ): Z and Y are swapped for all shortcuts

With "Kbd: DE" CTRL+Z (undo) is the key labelled Z, CTRL+Y (redo) the key labelled Y, and
SHIFT+Z (autoadvance mode) the key labelled Z - exactly as printed on the keyboard. The notes are
not affected: the lowest note row starts with the key left of X, which is labelled Y on a German
keyboard (Y S X D C V G B H N J M). The keyboard reference (F12) and the welcome window show the
note keys for the selected layout.

On the first start the layout is detected from the keyboard settings of the system. The choice
is saved with the other settings.

## A first song in five minutes

0. To hear what two SIDs sound like, open examples/solar_voyage_2sid.sng (Open or F10) and press
   F1. F4 stops.
1. Click New and choose the number of SIDs. Press F7 to go to the instrument editor. Click the
   Library button and choose a ready-made instrument (type a word to search, SPACE plays a
   preview, ENTER loads it), or click the dice button (CTRL+R) to create a random instrument.
2. Press F5 (or click into a pattern). The cursor is on the note column of voice 1.1.
3. Check that the status bar shows EDIT MODE (SPACE switches between edit and jam mode; in jam
   mode notes are only played, not written).
4. Type notes on the computer keyboard like on a piano: Z S X D C V G B H N J M (German
   keyboard: Y S X D C V G B H N J M) for the lower octave and Q 2 W 3 E R 5 T 6 Y 7 U (German:
   Q 2 W 3 E R 5 T 6 Z 7 U) for the upper octave. "/" and "*" on the keypad change the octave.
5. Press F1 to play and F4 to stop. CTRL+Z undoes a mistake, CTRL+Y redoes it.
6. Press F6 to edit the orderlist and add more patterns, F11 to save.
7. Click Audio to make a WAV or MP3 file, or Video for an MP4 video to share your song.

# Multi-SID songs

## Number of SIDs

A song can use 1, 2, 4 or 8 SID chips. Click SIDs 1, 2, 4 or 8 in the settings bar to change
it. Each SID adds three voices:

|  SIDs   Voices   Channel names        File format when saving
|  1      3        1.1 - 1.3            GoatTracker 2 .sng
|  2      6        1.1 - 2.3            GoatTracker 2 Stereo .sng
|  4      12       1.1 - 4.3            C64 GrooveGoat .ggt
|  8      24       1.1 - 8.3            C64 GrooveGoat .ggt

The channel name s.v means SID s, voice v. When the number of SIDs is reduced, the voices of
the removed SIDs are deleted (you are asked first if they contain data). New voices start with
an empty pattern in every subtune. With one SID the three voices are shown in the middle of the
pattern area.

The pattern editor shows six voices at a time. With 4 or 8 SIDs, use the SID tabs above the
patterns (SID 1-2, SID 3-4 ...) or BACKQUOTE to move to the other voices. The orderlist editor
scrolls in the same way.

## SID addresses

The standard addresses of the SID chips are:

|  SID 1 $D400   SID 2 $D420   SID 3 $D440   SID 4 $D460
|  SID 5 $D480   SID 6 $D4A0   SID 7 $D4C0   SID 8 $D4E0

Click the SID address button at the bottom right of the status bar to change the addresses. They
are stored in .ggt songs and used for PRG exports. Allowed are $D400-$D7E0 and $DE00-$DFE0 in
steps of $20. Each export asks for the addresses again, so you can make versions for different
hardware.

## How the SIDs work together

- Every SID has its own filter and master volume. The filter commands (AXY, BXY, CXY), the
  filtertable and the master volume command (DXY) of a voice affect the SID this voice belongs
  to. The channel bitmask in the filter control selects voices 1-3 of that SID.
- Synchronization and ring modulation work between the three voices of one SID, as on real
  hardware.
- The tempo commands (FXY with $03-$7F and EXY funktempo) change the tempo of all voices of all
  SIDs; FXY with $83-$FF only changes the current voice.
- In stereo mode the SIDs are placed in the stereo field: with 2 SIDs one left and one right,
  with 4 and 8 SIDs spread over the whole stereo field (odd SIDs left, even SIDs right). The
  stereo width (100%, 90%, 80% or 70%) of the Stereo button narrows the field for the editor
  playback.
  Exported SID files contain no panning; the player decides.
- SHIFT+1,2,3 in the pattern editor mutes voice 1-3 of the SID of the current voice; in the
  orderlist editor it swaps the orderlist with voice 1-3 of the same SID.

## Limits

- Subtunes: up to 32 in the editor. The multi-SID playroutine supports
  256 / voices subtunes: 42 with 2 SIDs, 21 with 4 SIDs and 10 with 8 SIDs.
- Patterns: 208, instruments: 63, table rows: 255 per table. These limits are shared by all
  voices, as in GoatTracker.
- CPU time: on a real C64 the playroutine needs roughly 1000-2000 cycles per SID and frame,
  depending on the effects used. 8 SIDs fit into one PAL frame at 1x speed (about 8000 of 19656
  cycles), but 8 SIDs at 2x speed or more will usually be too slow for a real C64.

## Undo and redo

Every change of patterns, orderlists, instruments, tables and editor settings can be undone with
CTRL+Z or the Undo button in the status bar. The last 20 steps are kept. CTRL+Y (or
CTRL+SHIFT+Z, or the Redo button) brings back what was undone. A new change after an undo clears
the redo steps, as in other programs.

Loading an instrument from the library or creating a random instrument is one undo step,
including the table data. Loading a song clears the undo history.

With a German keyboard switch the status bar button to "Kbd: DE" so that CTRL+Z and CTRL+Y are
the keys labelled Z and Y (see "Keyboard layout").

### Note entry modes

The key mode (Keys: button in the status bar, or -K command line option) selects how notes are
entered:

- Tracker (default): two piano octaves on the keyboard (key positions of an English keyboard;
  on a German keyboard the key left of X is labelled Y).
      Lower octave: S D  G H J  L       Upper octave: 2 3  5 6 7  9 0
                   Z X CV B N M , .                 Q W ER T Y UI O P
- DMC: one octave (A W S E D F T G Y H U J K O L P); the number keys 0-7 select the octave.
- Janko: a Janko-style keyboard layout on two rows.

Autoadvance modes: advance after notes and data bytes (all), advance only after notes, or no
automatic advance. The step size (Step) sets how many rows the cursor moves.

## Orderlist editor (F6)

|  0-9, A-F     Enter pattern numbers
|  < >          Select subtune
|  - +          Insert transpose down / up command
|  SHIFT+R      Insert repeat command
|  SPACE        Set the start position for F2 (SHIFT+SPACE: on all voices)
|  BACKSPACE    Set the end position for F2
|  RETURN       Go to the pattern (SHIFT+RETURN: on all voices)
|  SHIFT+CURSOR Mark orderlist data
|  SHIFT+L      Mark / unmark the whole orderlist
|  SHIFT+X C V  Cut, copy, paste orderlist data
|  SHIFT+1 2 3  Swap the orderlist with voice 1-3 of the same SID

The orderlist contains pattern numbers ($00-$CF), transpose commands (+X / -X, up to 14
half steps up or 15 down), repeat commands (RX, repeats the following pattern 1-16 times, R0 =
16 times) and the RST end mark with the restart position.

Rules: if both transpose and repeat come before a pattern, the transpose must come first. The
last entry before RST must be a pattern number. Transpose is only reset when the song starts,
not when it loops.

|  0-9, A-F     Enter parameters
|  - + / < >    Select instrument
|  SPACE        Play a test note (SHIFT+SPACE silences it)
|  RETURN       Go to the table row of the wave/pulse/filter/vibrato pointer
|  SHIFT+RETURN Set the table pointer to an empty position or convert an old vibrato parameter
|  SHIFT+N      Edit the instrument name
|  SHIFT+S      Smart paste: move an instrument and renumber it in all patterns

|  CTRL+R       Random instrument generator (dice button in the instrument header)

The test note plays on the voice where the cursor was last in the pattern editor. To hear a

### Instrument parameters

|  Attack/Decay     $0 is the fastest attack or decay, $F the slowest
|  Sustain/Release  Sustain level $0 silent ... $F loudest; release like attack/decay
|  Wavetable Pos    Start row in the wavetable ($00 stops the wavetable)
|  Pulsetable Pos   Start row in the pulsetable ($00 leaves the pulse unchanged)
|  Filtertable Pos  Start row in the filtertable ($00 leaves the filter unchanged)
|  Vibrato Param    Speedtable index of the instrument vibrato
|  Vibrato Delay    Ticks until the instrument vibrato starts ($00 = no vibrato)
|  HR/Gate Timer    Ticks before the next note when gate off and hard restart happen.
|                   At most tempo-1. Bit $80 disables hard restart, bit $40 disables gate off
|  1stFrame Wave    Waveform of the first frame of a note, usually $09 (gate + test bit).
|                   $00, $FE and $FF keep the waveform and set the gate unchanged / off / on

### ADSR envelope graph

Below the instrument parameters the volume envelope of the instrument is drawn as a graph:

      V      /\            |<- gate bit cleared (key off) here
      O     /  \           |
      L    /    \__________|
      U   /                |\
      M  /                 | \
      E /                  |  \
    TIME ---------------------------->
          A   D      S       R

The rising line is the attack, the falling curve the decay down to the sustain level, the flat
part the sustain (as long as the note is held) and the dotted line marks the key off, after
which the release fades the sound out. The horizontal scale grows with the parameter values (the
real SID times grow roughly exponentially), so longer times are shown wider. Next to the graph
the real times of the SID are shown:

|  Value  Attack   Decay/Release       Value  Attack   Decay/Release
|  0      2 ms     6 ms                8      100 ms   300 ms
|  1      8 ms     24 ms               9      250 ms   750 ms
|  2      16 ms    48 ms               A      500 ms   1.5 s
|  3      24 ms    72 ms               B      800 ms   2.4 s
|  4      38 ms    114 ms              C      1 s      3 s
|  5      56 ms    168 ms              D      3 s      9 s
|  6      68 ms    204 ms              E      5 s      15 s
|  7      80 ms    240 ms              F      8 s      24 s

Click on the graph to edit the Attack/Decay value. The same graph is shown in the instrument
library and in the random instrument generator.

Examples: A/D 09 S/R 00 starts at full volume and fades out. A/D 00 S/R 8A drops quickly to
sustain level 8 and fades with release A after key off. A/D CC S/R AF rises slowly, decays
slowly to level A and fades out very slowly after key off.

Legato: with bit $40 in the HR/Gate Timer no hard restart or gate off happens. With 1stFrame
Wave $00 the gate is also left untouched, but the table pointers and ADSR are initialized.

## Instrument library

C64 GrooveGoat comes with a library of more than 5000 ready-made instruments ("SIDGen
instruments for GoatTracker 2") in two sets, one for the old SID (6581) and one for the new SID
(8580), sorted into categories such as bass, lead, drums, pads, strings and effects.

Open the library with the Library button in the instrument header or with CTRL+L. The library
window has three parts: the categories on the left, the instruments in the middle and the
selected instrument with its ADSR graph on the right.

|  Typing        Search: all words must appear in the name or the category ("acid bass")
|  BACKSPACE     Delete the last character of the search text
|  ESC           Clear the search text; ESC again closes the library
|  UP / DOWN     Select an instrument (also PAGE UP/DOWN, HOME, END, mouse wheel)
|  LEFT / RIGHT  Previous / next category ("All instruments", "Favourites", categories)
|  TAB           Switch between the 6581 and the 8580 set (or click 6581 / 8580)
|  SPACE         Preview: plays the instrument on the cursor voice (or click on a name)
|  INS or *      Mark / unmark as favourite (or click the star in front of the name)
|  ENTER         Load the instrument into the current instrument slot (or double click)

A single click on an instrument selects it and plays it at once, so you can click through the
list and listen; a double click loads it.

The set of the SID model selected in the settings bar is shown first. Favourites are marked with
a star and collected in the "Favourites" category; they are saved in the configuration folder
(favourites.txt) and kept for the next start. The preview only works while the music is stopped,
so it never disturbs a playing song.

Every preview starts with a freshly reset SID: the filter is switched off, the master volume is
set to full, all voice registers of the SID are cleared, the pulse width is set to the middle and
the preview voice is unmuted. So an instrument always sounds the same in the preview, no matter
which instrument was played before (without the reset, a filter left over from a previous
instrument could make the next one inaudible). When the library is closed, the editor's mute
and filter settings are restored. The same applies to the preview in the file selector (Load
Ins, SPACE) and in the random instrument generator.

About 450 instruments of the original SIDGen library had an envelope that was already over
during the first frame of a note (Attack/Decay $x0 or $x1 with sustain 0), in which the test bit
of "1stFrame Wave 09" keeps the voice silent - they only produced a click, in the preview and in
songs. In the library that comes with C64 GrooveGoat their decay was set to the intended value
(the release value of the instrument), and a few very short percussion sounds got a slightly
longer decay.

### Replacing an instrument of a song

Loading an instrument into a slot that the song already uses replaces it everywhere in the song.
C64 GrooveGoat takes care that only this instrument changes and the rest of the song keeps
playing exactly as before:

- The table data of the new instrument is added at the end of the tables. The old table rows of
  the slot are only removed if nothing else uses them. Rows that are still used - by another
  instrument whose program runs into them, by a jump of another program, by a wavetable command
  or by a pattern command (e.g. a vibrato or portamento speed of 1XY-4XY, or a wave/pulse/filter
  program started with 8XY/9XY/AXY) - are kept. When rows are removed, all pointers to the
  rows behind them (instruments, jumps, wavetable and pattern commands) are moved along.
- The filter routing of the new instrument is adapted to the song: library instruments route all
  three voices of a SID through the filter, which would also filter the other voices of the song.
  When the instrument is loaded, the filter program only selects the voices on which the
  instrument is used in the song (all subtunes are checked); if it is not used yet, the voice of
  the pattern cursor.
- If the tables are too full for the new instrument, nothing is changed and the message line
  says so. Delete unused table rows first (SHIFT+O in the table editor optimizes a table).
- Previews never change the song: all instruments, tables and patterns are restored exactly after
  every preview and when the library is closed.

Loading an instrument is one undo step (CTRL+Z restores the old instrument and its tables).

Instruments can also be loaded with Load Ins (file selector, SPACE previews), and you can drop
.ins files onto the window to load them into the current slot. Instruments are saved and loaded
in the GoatTracker 2 .ins format, so they can be exchanged with GoatTracker.

The library folder ("instruments") is next to the program (Windows), inside the application
bundle (macOS) or inside the AppImage (Linux). Your own instruments can be stored anywhere.

## Random instrument generator

The dice button in the instrument header (or CTRL+R) opens the random instrument generator. It
builds complete instruments - ADSR, wavetable, pulsetable, filtertable and vibrato - from
recipes for typical C64 sounds. Every click on Generate creates a new variation.

### Sounds

|  Bass            Pulse bass with width sweep, slap bass, saw bass, octave "pop" bass
|  Acid bass       Saw or pulse through a resonant low-pass with a fast filter sweep
|  Bass drum       Noise click plus a fast falling triangle/pulse (absolute notes)
|  Snare           Noise, a short pulse "body" and a noise tail
|  Hi-hat          Closed or open hi-hat with high noise (optionally high-pass filtered)
|  Tom             Falling triangle or pulse tone
|  Woodwind        Flute (triangle with breath), clarinet (square wave), oboe (narrow pulse
|                  through a band-pass), with delayed vibrato
|  Guitar          Clean pluck, distorted lead guitar or a fast power chord
|  Brass           Sawtooth with a filter "swell" that opens at the start of the note
|  Strings         Slow attack, sawtooth or pulse width modulation, gentle filter movement
|  Lead            PWM lead, saw lead with filter, chip lead with octave start
|  Pad             Very slow attack and release, slow filter wobble
|  Arpeggio        Looping chords: major, minor, sus4, maj7, min7, fifth, octave
|  Bell / mallet   Bright start two octaves up, falling into a soft triangle
|  Effect          Laser, warble/siren, noise sweep

### SID filter options

|  SID filter       Auto uses the filter that fits the sound (or none). You can force: Off,
|                   Low-pass, Band-pass, High-pass, Low+band-pass, Notch (low+high-pass),
|                   Band+high-pass or all three.
|  Resonance        Auto or $0-$F
|  Cutoff           Auto, Low, Middle or High
|  Filter movement  Auto, Static, Sweep down, Sweep up or Wobble (up and down in a loop)
|  Filtered voice   The voices whose filter routing bit is set. "Auto" uses the voices on
|                   which the instrument slot is used in the song (or the voice of the
|                   pattern cursor for a new instrument); voice 1, 2 or 3 can be chosen.

The generator knows the SID model selected in the settings bar: the 6581 filter is darker and
opens at much lower cutoff values than the 8580, and combined waveforms (e.g. pulse+saw) are
only used for the 8580, because they are very quiet on the 6581.

### Keys

|  UP / DOWN     Select a line (sound, filter options, buttons)
|  LEFT / RIGHT  Change the selected option (a new instrument is generated)
|  R             Generate a new variation
|  SPACE         Preview (plays a typical note for the sound on the cursor voice)
|  ENTER         Use it: keep the instrument in the current slot (one undo step)
|  ESC           Cancel: the slot is restored as it was

"Use it" replaces the instrument in the slot everywhere in the song; the rest of the song stays
unchanged (see "Replacing an instrument of a song" in the instrument library chapter).

The preview only works while the music is stopped. The result area shows the name, the ADSR
values with the envelope graph and the generated table rows (left:right values). If the tables
are full, a message tells you to delete unused table data first (SHIFT+O in the table editor).

# Song data reference

## Pattern commands

|  0XY  Do nothing. The data byte is always $00.
|  1XY  Portamento up. XY is an index to a 16-bit speed in the speedtable.
|  2XY  Portamento down. XY is an index to a 16-bit speed in the speedtable.
|  3XY  Toneportamento to the note. XY is a speedtable index, or $00 for "tie note"
|       (move the pitch to the target note at once).
|  4XY  Vibrato. XY is a speedtable index: left side = speed (time until the direction
|       changes), right side = depth (pitch change per tick).
|  5XY  Set attack/decay to XY.
|  6XY  Set sustain/release to XY.
|  7XY  Set waveform to XY (no effect while the wavetable changes the waveform).
|  8XY  Set wavetable pointer ($00 stops the wavetable).
|  9XY  Set pulsetable pointer ($00 stops the pulsetable).
|  AXY  Set filtertable pointer ($00 stops the filtertable) of this voice's SID.
|  BXY  Set filter control: X = resonance, Y = voice bitmask of this SID. $00 switches the
|       filter off and stops the filtertable.
|  CXY  Set filter cutoff to XY.
|  DXY  X = 0: set the master volume of this SID to Y. X > 0: timing mark, XY is copied to
|       player address + $3F (only with "Store author-info").
|  EXY  Funktempo: XY is a speedtable index; the tempo alternates between the left and right
|       value on each row. Affects all voices.
|  FXY  Set tempo. $03-$7F sets the tempo of all voices, $83-$FF only of the current voice.
|       $00-$01 recall the funktempo values.

The master volume is $F by default and is only reset when a song is loaded or cleared, not when
playback starts. If you change it, reset it at the beginning of your song.

Without a command 1XY-4XY the instrument vibrato is active. The one-shot commands 5XY-FXY let a
previous 1XY-4XY command continue underneath them.

## Wavetable

|  Left side   00     Leave the waveform unchanged
|              01-0F  Delay this step by 1-15 frames
|              10-DF  Waveform register value
|              E0-EF  Inaudible waveforms $00-$0F (for example E9 = test bit + gate)
|              F0-FE  Execute pattern command 0XY-EXY, the right side is the parameter
|              FF     Jump to the row on the right side ($00 = stop)
|  Right side  00-5F  Relative note up
|              60-7F  Relative note down
|              80     Keep the frequency unchanged
|              81-DF  Absolute note C#0 - B-7

Waveform bits: 01 gate, 02 sync, 04 ring modulation, 08 test bit, 10 triangle, 20 sawtooth,
40 pulse, 80 noise. Sync and ring modulation use the previous voice of the same SID (voice 1
uses voice 3).

Examples:

|  01: 41 00    Flute: pulse on the first frame,
|  02: 11 00    then triangle.
|  03: FF 00
|
|  01: 81 D0    Snare drum with absolute notes (use pulse width $800).
|  02: 41 AA
|  03: 41 A4
|  04: 80 D4
|  05: 80 D1
|  06: FF 00
|
|  01: 41 00    Looping 4-note arpeggio with pulse waveform.
|  02: 00 04
|  03: 00 07
|  04: 00 0C
|  05: 00 00
|  06: FF 02

The pattern commands 0XY, 8XY and EXY can not be executed from the wavetable.

## Pulsetable

|  Left side   01-7F  Modulation step: left = time in ticks, right = signed speed
|              8X-FX  Set pulse width: X = high 4 bits, right side = low 8 bits
|              FF     Jump to the row on the right side ($00 = stop)

|  01: 80 10    Pulse $010 (very thin)
|  02: 20 40    32 ticks up with speed $40
|  03: 40 E0    64 ticks down with speed -$20
|  04: 40 20    64 ticks up with speed $20
|  05: FF 03    Loop back to row 03

## Filtertable

|  Left side   00     Set the cutoff to the right side value
|              01-7F  Modulation step: left = time, right = signed speed
|              80-F0  Set filter parameters: high nybble = passband ($90 lowpass,
|                     $A0 bandpass, $C0 highpass...), right = resonance / voice bitmask
|              FF     Jump to the row on the right side ($00 = stop)

"Set filter parameters" directly followed by "Set cutoff" are executed on the same frame. Each
SID has its own filter, so a filtertable started by a voice of SID 2 controls the filter of
SID 2.

|  01: 90 F1    Lowpass, resonance F, filter voice 1
|  02: 00 40    Cutoff $40
|  03: FF 00    Stop

## Speedtable

|  Vibrato      XX YY  XX = speed (time until direction changes), YY = depth
|  Portamento   XX YY  16-bit speed added to the frequency each tick (XX = high byte)
|  Funktempo    XX YY  Two tempos that alternate on each row, starting with XX

If XX has bit $80 set, vibrato depth / portamento speed are calculated note-independently and
YY is the divisor (number of right shifts). You can enter old style parameters in patterns and
instruments and press SHIFT+RETURN to convert them into speedtable entries.

## Playback details

Every pattern row is divided into "ticks" (frames) as given by the tempo. With tempo 6 and gate
timer 2:

|  Tick 0     New notes are initialized, orderlist advance, wavetable, one-shot commands
|  Tick 1     New notes become audible, pulsetable, wavetable or realtime commands
|  Tick 2,3   Pulsetable, wavetable or realtime commands
|  Tick 4     New notes are fetched, gate off and hard restart, no pulsetable
|  Tick 5     Pulsetable, wavetable or realtime commands

The filtertable runs on every tick. PO and RO in the settings bar switch off the pulse and
realtime command skipping (more CPU time in the exported player).

## Tips

- Patterns use less memory the fewer command changes they contain.
- Instrument vibrato can make 4XY commands unnecessary.
- Stop an unneeded pulse modulation with a pulse program "80 00 / FF 00" or command 900.
- The fastest tempo is normally 3; with funktempo "02 02", gate timer 1 and PO switched off you
  can reach tempo 2.
- Without any pattern commands, the start tempo can be set with the Attack/Decay value of
  instrument $3F (if it is otherwise unused).
- Multispeed songs: multiply the tempos and gate timers by the speed multiplier and adjust
  vibrato and modulation speeds.

# Files

## Song formats

|  .sng  GoatTracker 2 song (1 SID) - can be loaded by GoatTracker 2
|  .sng  GoatTracker 2 Stereo song (2 SIDs) - can be loaded by GoatTracker Stereo
|  .ggt  C64 GrooveGoat song (4 or 8 SIDs, or any number if you choose this extension)

The song is saved in the format that matches the number of SIDs. Songs with more than 2 SIDs
are always saved as .ggt, because the GoatTracker formats can not store them. When loading, the
number of voices of a .sng file is detected automatically. Older GoatTracker formats (GTS2-GTS4,
v1.x) can be loaded too.

The .ggt format: "GGT1", number of SIDs, flags (NTSC, 8580), speed multiplier, hard restart
ADSR, the SID addresses, followed by the GoatTracker 2 song data with one orderlist per voice.

## Instruments

Instruments use the GoatTracker 2 .ins format (GTI5). Older GoatTracker instruments are
converted on loading.

## Backup copies

C64 GrooveGoat protects your work in three ways:

- Saving keeps the previous version: before a song file is overwritten, the old file is copied
  to NAME.bak (for example mysong.sng.bak) in the same folder. To go back to it, rename it
  (remove ".bak") and load it.
- Automatic backup: while a song has unsaved changes, a copy is written every 2 minutes into
  the "autosave" folder in the configuration folder (see below). The file names start with the
  date and time (for example 20261020-143012_mysong.sng). The newest 10 copies are kept, older
  ones are deleted. The message line shows when a copy was made. Your song file itself is not
  touched by the automatic backup.
- Crash recovery: if the program was not closed properly (crash, power failure, killed
  process), the next start offers to restore the newest automatic backup. "Restore" loads it;
  saving then writes to the original file name again. "Ignore" starts normally - the backup
  stays in the autosave folder, so you can still load it later with Open.

## Configuration

Settings are saved when the program exits:

|  Windows  %APPDATA%\C64 GrooveGoat\groovegoat.cfg
|  macOS    ~/.groovegoat/groovegoat.cfg
|  Linux    ~/.groovegoat/groovegoat.cfg

The same folder contains favourites.txt (instrument library favourites) and the autosave folder
with the automatic backup copies.

# Export

C64 GrooveGoat can export your song for C64 players and emulators (SID file, runnable PRG,
music data for your own programs) and as audio or video files for everybody else (WAV, MP3,
MP4). All exports use the song exactly as it is in the editor; nothing is changed.

## Export dialog (SID, PRG, BIN)

The SID and PRG buttons (toolbar) and F9 open the export dialog:

|  Format           SID file, runnable PRG with player screen, PRG with music only, or BIN
|  Player address   Start address of the music (page aligned, default $1000)
|  Zeropage         Two zeropage bytes used by the playroutine (default $FC-$FD)
|  SID address(es)  Where the SID chips are (see below)
|  Options          Playroutine options (see below)

UP/DOWN selects a line, LEFT/RIGHT changes values, ENTER edits a value or starts the export.
After packing, the sizes of the parts are shown, then you choose the file name.

The packer removes unused patterns, instruments and table rows, duplicate table parts and all
playroutine code that the song does not need.

## SID files

C64 GrooveGoat writes PSID files as specified by the High Voltage SID Collection:

|  1 SID    PSID version 2
|  2 SIDs   PSID version 3; SID 2 can be at any address $D420-$D7E0 or $DE00-$DFE0
|  4/8 SIDs PSID version 5 (multi-SID format)

In SID files the first SID is always at $D400. For 4 and 8 SIDs the PSID v5 format describes
the addresses with a layout, which is chosen in the export dialog:

|  +$20 steps    $D400 $D420 $D440 $D460 $D480 $D4A0 $D4C0 $D4E0 (standard)
|  +$40 steps    $D400 $D440 $D480 $D4C0 ...
|  +$80 steps    $D400 $D480 $D500 $D580 ...
|  +$100 steps   $D400 $D500 $D600 $D700 ...
|  +$20/+$60     $D400 $D420 $D480 $D4A0 ...
|  +$20/+$E0     $D400 $D420 $D500 $D520 ...
|  +$100/+$300   $D400 $D500 $DE00 $DF00

"SID 2 starts at" moves the second and all following SIDs. Addresses beyond $D7E0 continue at
$DE00. If your song's SID addresses match one of the layouts, it is selected automatically.

Song name, author and released are stored in the header (at most 31 characters each). The SID
model and PAL/NTSC are stored in the flags, multispeed songs contain a CIA timer setup.

## Runnable PRG

"PRG - runnable" creates a C64 program that starts with RUN:

    LOAD"SONGNAME",8,1
    RUN

The player screen shows the C64 GrooveGoat logo (an eighth note in front of the C64 rainbow),
the tracker name, version and author, the song name, author and released text, the current
subtune, its length and the play time, and the number, model and addresses of the SIDs.

|  + or CURSOR RIGHT   next subtune
|  - or CURSOR LEFT    previous subtune
|  1-9                 select subtune 1-9
|  SPACE               restart the subtune

The player screen occupies $0801 up to about $1000, so the music must start at $1000 or higher
and end below $D000. The song length is calculated by playing the subtune until all voices have
reached their loop point (at most 30 minutes).

## Music-only PRG and BIN

"PRG - music only" contains the packed music and playroutine with a load address, "BIN" the same
without load address. Use them in your own programs:

    LDA #subtune      ; starting from 0
    JSR address       ; init
    ...
    JSR address+3     ; play, once per frame (or more often for multispeed)

With sound effect support the sound effect routine is at address+6. The volume routine is at
address+6, or at address+9 when sound effect support is also enabled.

## Playroutine options

|  Buffered SID-writes     Write each voice's registers in one go. More stable sound, more
|                          memory and CPU time.
|  Sound effect support    Adds a sound effect engine for games (implies buffered writes):
|                          LDA #<effect, LDY #>effect, LDX #voice, JSR address+6.
|                          Voice index: 0, 7, 14 (1 SID); sid*32 + voice*7 (multi-SID).
|  Volume change support   Adds a routine to set the master volume (of all SIDs):
|                          LDA #volume, JSR address+6 (+9 with sound effects).
|  Store author-info       Stores the author text at address+$20 and enables timing marks.
|  Use zeropage ghostregs  (1 SID only) The playroutine writes to zeropage instead of the SID.
|  Disable optimization    Keep all playroutine code (only needed in case of trouble).
|  Full SID buffering      Copy all registers of the previous frame at the start of each
|                          play call (maximum stability, one frame latency).

## Multi-SID playroutine

Songs with 2 or more SIDs use the C64 GrooveGoat multi-SID playroutine, an extension of the
GoatTracker 2 playroutine. It plays all voices of all SIDs; every SID has its own filter and
volume. It produces exactly the same SID register values as the GoatTracker playroutine plays
for a single SID.

If the SIDs are $20 apart (the standard layout), the registers are written directly. For any
other layout the playroutine writes into a register buffer, which is copied to the SIDs at the
beginning of the next play call (one frame latency, like "Full SID buffering"). The buffer is
placed at the end of the music data.

If the hard restart attack is $F, the alternative SID register write order is used (waveform
before ADSR), as in GoatTracker.

## Audio export (WAV, MP3)

The Audio button (or CTRL+F9) renders the song with the SID emulation into an audio file. The
rendering is done offline - much faster than real time - with exactly the settings of the editor
(SID model, PAL/NTSC, number of SIDs, stereo placement, reSID/reSID-fp and interpolation).
Stereo files always use the full stereo width (100%); the width setting of the Stereo button
only applies to the playback in the editor.

|  Format         WAV audio (16 bit PCM), MP3 audio or MP4 video
|  Subtune        Which subtune to render
|  Length         "Song length": until all voices reach their loop point (calculated
|                 automatically) plus the fade-out; or "Custom" with your own length
|  Custom length  LEFT/RIGHT change by 1 second (SHIFT: 10 seconds), ENTER lets you type
|                 the length as m:ss or in seconds (at most 60 minutes)
|  Fade-out       Off, 2, 3, 5, 8 or 10 seconds. The song keeps playing (it loops) while
|                 it fades out softly. Not used when the song ends by itself.
|  Channels       Stereo or mono
|  Sample rate    44100 Hz (CD) or 48000 Hz
|  MP3 bitrate    128, 160, 192, 256 or 320 kbit/s (constant bitrate)
|  Single voices  WAV only: also save every voice as its own mono WAV file
|  Keep muted     Normally all voices are exported. With this option voices that are muted
|                 in the editor (M in the voice header, SHIFT+F4) stay silent.

The summary at the bottom shows the length, the format and the estimated file size. ENTER or
Export asks for the file name; a progress window is shown while rendering and ESC cancels (the
unfinished file is deleted).

Single voice files: with "Also save every voice as its own mono WAV" C64 GrooveGoat writes the
normal mix and one extra file per voice: NAME_voice1.wav ... NAME_voice3.wav for one SID, and
NAME_sid1_voice1.wav ... NAME_sid8_voice3.wav for several SIDs. Every voice file has the same
length, so they line up perfectly in an audio editor or DAW (for remixing, mastering or
analysing a song). Voice files are mono, at full level and without stereo placement. The
filter of each SID is applied to its voices as in the mix; voice 3 "off" and sync/ring
modulation keep working, because the other voices of the SID still run - they are only silenced
at the output.

MP3 files get ID3 tags with the song name (title), the author (artist), the year from the
"released" text, the genre "Electronic" and a comment "Made with C64 GrooveGoat". The MP3
encoder is LAME (quality setting 2, joint stereo).

Tip: exported audio is not affected by the volume slider. The levels of the SIDs are the same
as in the editor at 100% volume, with a soft limiter that prevents hard clipping.

## Video export (MP4)

The Video button (or CTRL+SHIFT+F9) renders an MP4 video of the song, ready for YouTube, social
media or a presentation:

- 1280 x 720 pixels (720p), 50 frames per second (PAL) or 60 (NTSC), H.264 video
- AAC audio (48 kHz, stereo or mono, about 192 kbit/s)
- a header with the logo, the song name, author, SID model, PAL/NTSC, subtune, the play time
  and the total length, and a progress bar in the C64 rainbow colours
- one oscilloscope for every voice, arranged to fit the number of SIDs:

|  1 SID    3 oscilloscopes, one per row (full width)
|  2 SIDs   6 oscilloscopes: SID 1 left, SID 2 right, one voice per row
|  4 SIDs   12 oscilloscopes: one SID per row, voices 1-3 side by side
|  8 SIDs   24 oscilloscopes: 4 rows with two SIDs (2 x 3 voices) each

Every oscilloscope shows the waveform of its voice (after the envelope, before the filter), its
name (SID 1 - Voice 1 or S1 - V1), the current waveform (TRI, SAW, PUL with pulse width, NOISE,
combinations), whether the voice is filtered, a gate indicator and a bar with the envelope level.
The display is triggered on the rising zero crossing, so periodic waveforms stand still. Voice 1
is drawn in red, voice 2 in yellow and voice 3 in blue.

The dialog offers the same options as the audio export (subtune, length, fade-out, stereo/mono,
muted voices) plus the video colours: "Night" (C64 blue, the default) or "Day" (light). The
song name and author come from the song name editor (F8). Rendering a video takes roughly half
the playing time on a modern computer (more with 8 SIDs); the progress window shows the state,
ESC cancels.

The video is stored with BT.709 colour information, so the colours look the same in every
player. The MP4 files play in all common players, web browsers and on phones.

## Command line rendering

Audio and video files can also be made without opening a window:

    groovegoat song.sng --render=song.wav
    groovegoat song.sng --render=song.mp3 --bitrate=256
    groovegoat song.ggt --render=song.mp4 --fade=8
    groovegoat song.sng --render=song.wav --stems --seconds=90

The format is chosen by the extension (.wav, .mp3 or .mp4). See "Command line".

## Sound output

The editor plays the sound through the default audio device of the system. The SID emulation
runs in its own thread, a little ahead of the sound card, so other programs (a web browser with
a video, an image viewer) do not cause crackling. If you still hear dropouts on a very slow or
busy computer, start the program with a bigger buffer, for example -B200 (200 milliseconds).
The default sample rate is 48000 Hz; -M44100 selects 44100 Hz.
